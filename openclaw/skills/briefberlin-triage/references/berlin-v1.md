# Berlin relevance, version berlin-v1

State what changed, who in Berlin is affected, why the timing matters and which specific fact supports the judgment. Distinguish an interested party's claim, a decision, money committed and an effect already observed. A Berlin imprint or a shared court name alone supplies little evidence of a Berlin consequence. A neighbourhood event can merit wide coverage through its human impact. Score 0–10 in whole numbers, then separately judge evidence sufficiency and expiry.

| Score | Editorial meaning |
|---|---|
| 9–10 | Urgent, verified change with major consequences for Berlin readers. The dispatcher may select it immediately. |
| 7–8 | Significant local story or well-established impact on Berlin households. Highest eligible story competes for the next 12-hour slot. |
| 4–6 | Plausible reader interest with narrower reach or a modest consequence. The editor chooses whether to publish. |
| 0–3 | Routine, incidental or outside Berlin with no demonstrated local consequence. Retain the decision for repeat matching. |

Use precise attribution. Two outlets quoting the same dpa dispatch supply one reporting origin. Keep both URLs and credited origins through separate sightings. A document and an independently obtained witness account can support different parts of an event. State each fact's source. Syndication, larger agencies and additional announcements alone merit no score increase.

For an unchanged event, choose `same` with `material=false` and name the concrete match. For genuinely changed facts affecting the same pending event, use `same` and explain the changed decision or reader consequence. A factual error is a `correction`. A later action, such as awarding a contract after an invitation to tender, may be a `follow-up` with a new identity and a parent. For an ambiguous candidate or conflicting accounts, hold the report. No elapsed-time cutoff rules out a distinctive older match.
