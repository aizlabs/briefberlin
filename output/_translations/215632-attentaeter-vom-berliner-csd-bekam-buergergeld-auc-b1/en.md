In July 2026, an Islamist attacked Christopher Street Day in Berlin. It is a large parade for the rights of queer people. It has now emerged that the attacker, Abdul B., received welfare benefits for months in 2025. These are payments from the state for people without enough income. At the same time, however, he was in Lebanon and spent some of that time in prison. From May to November 2025, he was paid 790.61 euros every month. This was stated in a response from the Bundesarbeitsministerium to a question from AfD member of parliament René Springer. The newspaper Bild was the first to report it.

On 20 May 2025, the then 20-year-old travelled to Lebanon. He wanted to join the terrorist group Islamischer Staat. He was arrested there in July and convicted by a court. On 17 November 2025, he was released and returned to Germany. There he was held in pre-trial detention, so he received no money during that time. After his conviction in May 2026, he was initially free again. He then received benefits once more: a partial payment of 524.55 euros from 12 May, and 804.50 euros a month from June. According to the ministry, he had been receiving basic income support since January 2015, when he was still a child. On 25 July 2026, the 21-year-old attacked the CSD event. One woman died and more than two dozen people were injured, some seriously. A day later, police shot the attacker dead.

The ministry says the job centre responsible knew nothing about the six months abroad. Other authorities knew about the case, however: staff from the German embassy visited him in prison. According to Bild, the job centre invited him to appointments several times during this period. He never came, however. Instead, someone is said to have sent sick notes from Germany. It is still unclear who sent them and which doctors issued them.

Springer speaks of a “monstrous failure of the state”. He is particularly critical of Federal Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt of the CSU. Dobrindt likes to present himself as tough on security issues, Springer says. Yet his authorities did not inform the job centre. Springer therefore holds Dobrindt responsible for a terrorist receiving welfare benefits. The case raises important questions: How well do security and welfare authorities share information?
