The trial of former RBB chief Patricia Schlesinger is beginning at the Landgericht Berlin. RBB is the public broadcaster for Berlin and Brandenburg. The public prosecutor's office accuses Schlesinger of causing financial harm to the broadcaster in 26 cases. Wolf-Dieter Wolf, Hagen Brandstäter and Susann Lange are also on trial. All four deny the accusations. A total of 56 days of hearings are planned for the trial.

Wolf was formerly head of the administrative board, which oversees RBB's management. Brandstäter headed administration, while Lange headed the legal department. According to the public prosecutor's office, the defendants acted in different groups. One key issue is special payments to senior staff. Around 933,500 euros are alleged to have been paid out without justification. A further roughly 122,400 euros related to the period when RBB chaired ARD, the association of public broadcasters. Schlesinger and Wolf are alleged to have deliberately bypassed the administrative board when making the special payments.

Other accusations concern ten dinners at Schlesinger's apartment costing around 4,871 euros. The case also involves a trip to London with her husband costing about 1,650 euros. The court will also consider arrangements for two employees. One was allowed to retire early, while the other no longer had to work. The public prosecutor's office considers the allegations particularly serious in seven cases.

The affair sparked a crisis at RBB in the summer of 2022. It began with questions about a consulting contract for Schlesinger's husband at Messe Berlin. Wolf was head of the supervisory board there and at the same time head of RBB's administrative board. Later, Schlesinger also faced criticism over her company car and the expensive renovation of her office floor. She considered her dinner invitations to be work-related. One guest, however, thought it was a private gathering. Schlesinger resigned on 7 August 2022. On 22 August, RBB dismissed her without notice.

The affair damaged trust in public broadcasting. A committee of the Brandenburger Landtag examined the work and duties of the administrative board. Stricter professional requirements now apply to its members. The court must now determine whether the defendants broke criminal law. Schlesinger's defense is calling for an acquittal, saying that her decisions were intended to serve the broadcaster.
