On Friday, thousands of young people took to the streets again in Berlin and many other German cities. The initiative “Gen Z regelt” had called for the protests. It is firmly against compulsory military service, meaning mandatory service in the Bundeswehr. It also calls for more money for schools, addiction support and psychotherapy, affordable living and greater climate protection.

In Berlin, the protest march started at Alexanderplatz at around 1:30 p.m., two hours late. It went to Mariannenplatz in Kreuzberg. The slogan was: “Fight together instead of shooting at each other!” The march was registered for 5,000 people. Police spoke of about 4,000 participants, while the organisers said there were between 10,000 and 12,000. According to police, everything remained peaceful in the afternoon. Up to 1,600 police officers were on duty day and night. Alongside many young school students, left-wing and communist parties and pro-Palestinian groups also joined the march. At the start, a youth orchestra played “Bella Ciao” among other songs.

At the closing rally, a 24-year-old criticised the fact that billions are going into military buildup while universities are falling apart. A girl of about ten accused Chancellor Friedrich Merz of pursuing right-wing policies. Grünen leader Felix Banaszak supported the young people. Life is becoming more and more expensive, he said. And then, on your 18th birthday, an invitation to a Bundeswehr military assessment arrives in your mailbox. This puts pressure on the younger generation.

At the same time, the Bundesschülerkonferenz organised its own rally at Brandenburger Tor. This event was also registered for 5,000 people. The group is deliberately distancing itself from “Gen Z regelt”. Its main concern is education: renovated schools, more social work, less stress for teachers and climate protection in lessons. The Bundeselternrat, trade unions and environmental groups support it.

Co-organiser Akram Selo had already been putting pressure on decision-makers. Talks alone are not enough, he said. The movement wants firm commitments instead of empty promises. So far, it does not feel that it has been taken seriously. For Friday, the group counted nearly 21,000 registrations in 73 cities. At the end of October, there were still over 180,000. At that time, tens of thousands demonstrated mostly peacefully, but there were riots in several cities in the evening, for example in Frankfurt. Selo stressed that the group wants peaceful protests. Those looking for violence are not to be found at its demonstrations.
