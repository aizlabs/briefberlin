At Berlin’s Charité, the dispute over better conditions for nursing staff is growing. In a vote by the trade union Verdi, 98 percent of participants voted for an indefinite strike. They want to stop working if the talks with the hospital management fail. The issue is a new contract that is meant to relieve nursing staff when there is too little staff.

The contract sets out what happens when too few nursing staff are working on a ward. Then the employees can receive days off, money, or other benefits, for example. Many nursing staff at Charité are organized in Verdi. That is why a long strike would carry great weight for the hospital.

The employees won the previous contract in 2021 after a tough conflict. Later, it became a model for other hospitals. However, the board of Charité terminated the contract at the end of the year. It gives the difficult financial situation as the reason. Charité belongs to the Land Berlin. Recently, Charité posted a clear loss. In addition, new federal rules for hospitals could increase costs. The Land Berlin has also cut money from the science sector. This could already affect the planning of study places.

There has already been an agreement with the Marburger Bund. This organization represents the doctors. It agreed with Charité on higher salaries and shift allowances, meaning extra money for certain working hours. The dispute with Verdi, however, remains unresolved. In recent weeks, Verdi has several times called short warning strikes in individual areas. Further talks are planned for next week. If Verdi officially declares the talks to have failed, an indefinite strike can begin.

Such a strike would have serious consequences for hospital operations. Depending on participation, Charité would have to postpone planned treatments. That would affect patients and reduce income. Charité is Europe’s largest university hospital. It has locations in Mitte, Wedding, Steglitz, and Pankow. Every year it treats hundreds of thousands of people, as outpatients and in the hospital. With around 25,000 employees, it is very important for health care in Berlin. This includes subsidiaries and about 7,000 nursing staff. That is why the conflict is especially politically sensitive.
