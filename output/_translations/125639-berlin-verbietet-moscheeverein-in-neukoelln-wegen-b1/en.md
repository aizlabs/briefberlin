Berlin’s Senat banned a mosque association in Neukölln on Tuesday. The association “El-Irschad” is alleged to have supported the Lebanese Hisbollah. Three organisations belonging to the association have also been banned with immediate effect: “Al-Qaem”, “Al-Thaqalyn” and “Qalam Bildungszentrum”. From six in the morning, police searched their premises in Neukölln. These included the community centre on Flughafenstraße, which many people across Germany know as a mosque. Around 40 police officers took part in the operation, which remained fairly calm. At the same time, investigators searched the homes of known members in other Berlin districts.

Interior senator Iris Spranger (SPD) explained the ban. She said the association works against understanding between peoples and violates criminal law. According to Spranger, “El-Irschad” supported Hisbollah with organisation and money. This also allowed the militia to spread its propaganda against the German constitution among Shia Muslims in Berlin, for example through youth work and in the mosque. Members are no longer allowed to meet as they did before. The authorities have seized the association’s assets, including the money in its accounts. Its symbols are banned in public, and its websites are being shut down.

The name Hisbollah means “Party of God”. It is the strongest Shia force in the Middle East and is very close to the government in Iran. In Lebanon, it is part of the government. Even so, for decades it controlled the south and east of the country almost like a state of its own. Recently, Israeli attacks have severely weakened it. All work for Hisbollah has been banned in Germany since March 2020. At that time, Federal Interior Minister Horst Seehofer (CSU) classified it as a terrorist organisation. The domestic intelligence agency counts more than 1,000 supporters in Germany. The militia receives money from Lebanon and Iran, as well as donations from supporters abroad. It probably also earns money through crime.

The Shia centre on Harzer Straße in Neukölln was not affected by the operation. The association there runs a mosque and a day care centre. But Berlin politicians have long criticised it. It follows the prominent Iraqi cleric Ali al-Sistani. He honoured Hisbollah leader Hassan Nasrallah, who was killed in 2024, as a martyr.
