In Berlin, Linke, Grüne and SPD are currently negotiating to form a government together. The state’s difficult financial situation is becoming an increasing problem. The financial experts from the three parties have therefore set clear rules for all working groups. Every project that goes beyond the firm agreements made in the first talks must have secure funding. The money can come from other parts of the budget, from new revenue or from funds that are certain to be available. The rules are set out in a kind of guide that all negotiators have received.

This is a major obstacle for the politicians working on specific policy areas, as the partners have many wishes, some of them very expensive. First, the financial experts want to secure funding for the agreements already made. Together, these cost about 220 million euros per year. They include a rent cap at state-owned housing companies and an office to monitor rents. Other plans include a four-point plan for the economy and more money for climate protection and climate-friendly heating. People on low incomes are to receive a social ticket for nine euros and a cheaper Deutschlandticket. Other measures include help for people with addictions, drug consumption rooms, cleaner streets, a neighbourhood canteen, a youth culture card and a free museum Sunday. Police and fire stations are also to be renovated, and more cycle paths and bus lanes built. There are also agreements on public ownership, the underground railway, climate payments from a special fund and the children’s hospital at Charité. The working groups are to calculate exactly when the costs of each measure will arise.

All other wishes are possible only if the working groups find the money themselves. Anyone planning something new must therefore save money in their own area. In particular, older CDU projects are to be reviewed if they do not fit the new coalition’s goals. Symbolic cuts are also wanted. They are intended to show that the money really reaches citizens.

But there is no easy way out: the groups may not name higher taxes or fees as a source of money. The state needs this revenue to keep the overall budget stable in the long term. Projects without secure funding are therefore possible only in exceptional cases with good justification.
