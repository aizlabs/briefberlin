The death of 13-year-old Lisa T. from Berlin-Marzahn has shocked many people across Germany. It also started a discussion about a lack of courage to help. The reason was a report from the village of Altglietzen in Brandenburg, near the Polish border. Residents there were said to have heard cries for help but not called the police. The girl had been missing for several days. On a Sunday afternoon, police officers found her dead, largely by chance, on a farm in the village of 437 residents. Investigators suspect a 22-year-old German man with a criminal record. He had already been under investigation for child pornography for years. It is still unclear exactly how serious the crime was.

The story first came from a single report in a tabloid newspaper on Sunday evening. Many media outlets repeated it, even though they had not spoken to the neighbours themselves. On Monday, dozens of reporters came to the village and heard a different story. None of the neighbours they spoke to had heard cries for help, not even those living right next to the farm. Instead of indifference, the people showed shock and grief. On Tuesday, the Berlin public prosecutor's office also said there was no evidence of the screams so far. Investigators had previously knocked on many doors, apparently without success.

Even so, the claim was not completely made up. According to information from security sources, a married couple told investigators about screams on Sunday evening. Their house is near a path through the woods. The perpetrator may have dragged the girl along this path to the farm. It is not clear whether the couple remember correctly. Sometimes, after a crime, witnesses believe they noticed something. They are not deliberately lying, but imagine it afterwards. It is also possible, however, that the couple really did hear Lisa. That leaves the question of why they did not call for help. The two have not yet commented on this.

What is clear, however, is that the accusation on social media that an entire village looked away is not true. While many people online were already judging the village, numerous residents gathered in the church in Altglietzen. There, they mourned together, laid down candles and flowers, and remembered the girl who had been killed.
