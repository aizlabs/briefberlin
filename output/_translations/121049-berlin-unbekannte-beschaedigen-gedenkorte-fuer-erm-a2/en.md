On November 9, unknown people damaged three memorial sites in Berlin. These places remember Jews from the time of the Nazis. The RIAS office collects information about hate against Jews. It received three reports.

November 9 is an important day in Germany. On November 9, 1938, the Nazis attacked Jews everywhere. Memorial stones are small metal plates in the sidewalk. They have the names of Jews on them. The Nazis hunted or killed these people.

In Berlin-Mitte, someone put ketchup on a memorial stone. The name can no longer be read. The people who did this also damaged flowers. In Kreuzberg, people crossed out the stones for the Israel family with white paint. In front of them, they wrote “Palastine.”

At Hermannplatz, there is a poster with a photo of Arno Althoff. Someone scratched his eyes. Someone also put stickers with the Palestinian flag over his eyes and mouth. Arno Althoff was Jewish and was born in Berlin in 1919. His family lost their shop in Neukölln. After that, the Nazis forced him to do hard work. In 1942, he had a safe hiding place. But first he said goodbye to his parents. Then the Nazis sent him to Riga in Latvia. After that, no one knows what happened to him. Today, there is a memorial stone for him at Hermannstraße 123.

Julia Kopp leads a project at RIAS. She says: The people who do this are attacking the memory of the murdered Jews on purpose. They choose days of remembrance on purpose. Kopp thinks this is very bad. She also says: Some people compare Israel to the Nazis. In this way, they make victims into people who do harm.

Last year, RIAS counted 61 such cases at memorial sites in Berlin. This year too, people have damaged memorial sites in almost all districts. They often compare this with the war in Gaza. RIAS helps the people affected.
