The hacker attack on Berlin's state administration is apparently much bigger than previously known. The Senat, Berlin's state government, still cannot give a reliable figure. Now an analysis by Tagesspiegel Innovation Lab shows for the first time that more than 540,000 people are affected. Their names appear in spreadsheets, emails and databases that are now being shared on the dark web. This is a hidden part of the internet. The researchers used a cautious estimate, so the real number could be even higher. In numerical terms, that is almost one in seven Berlin residents. But the data also includes people from other German states and from abroad.

For more than 370,000 affected people, the researchers found at least one email address. These include work addresses belonging to employees of public offices and companies, as well as many private accounts. Often, however, more than just email addresses are exposed. For around 260,000 people, or about half, there is further information that can clearly be linked to an individual. For example, the birth dates of over 157,000 people can now be read freely, including in staff lists, job applications and other applications.

The data also contains phone numbers from email signatures, spreadsheets and job applications. The researchers were able to link around 34,000 mobile phone numbers to individual people. This is a major risk because scammers can use such numbers for phishing. They use false messages to try to obtain personal data or money. Bank details are even more dangerous: the researchers found a private IBAN, the number of a personal bank account, for just under 14,000 people.

The worst cases involve particularly sensitive information. For at least 1,000 people, the leak includes health data. This includes medical certificates, information about severe disabilities and documents about returning to work after a long illness. This group is relatively small. But it shows how seriously the attack violates people's privacy. Anyone who wants to know whether their own data is affected can check online. To do this, they enter their email address into a special checking tool. The pressure on the Senat to openly explain the extent of the attack itself is now likely to grow.
