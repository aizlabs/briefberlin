The Neuruppin public prosecutor’s office, which specialises in corruption, is investigating two employees of the Teltow-Fläming district administration in Brandenburg. They worked in the department that grants permits to private hire car companies. The two are suspected of taking money and granting permits in return, even though they were not allowed to do so. The companies offer cheap rides with a driver that can be booked through apps such as Uber and Bolt. The authorities are also investigating seven other people who are suspected of paying the money. Almost all of them are from Berlin. All suspects are presumed innocent until a verdict is reached.

The case highlights a problem that has worried experts for some time. In 2024, Berlin authorities took 1,661 app-based vehicles off the road because they were not properly registered with the responsible state office. Criminals had used this to avoid paying taxes. After that, many companies had to close, while many new businesses suddenly appeared in the surrounding area of Brandenburg. Because rides are booked through apps, their cars are still operating in Berlin. Today, many private hire cars with Brandenburg number plates can be seen there. Taxi associations and transport experts warned early on that undeclared work and tax fraud were simply moving elsewhere.

The district south of Berlin, which has around 177,000 residents, has come under particular criticism. This is shown by an answer from the state government to a question from SPD parliamentarian Martina Maxi Schmidt. From the end of 2025 to the middle of 2026, the number of private hire cars there almost doubled to more than 2,100. In Berlin, which has nearly four million residents, only 1,656 such vehicles were registered, according to the latest figures from March.

The investigation began in August, apparently after the district administration itself raised the issue. A spokesperson said that internal checks and information from employees had alerted the authority to the case. She described the matter as very sensitive. A thorough investigation is very important to the administration. The two employees have since been placed on leave.

The public prosecutor’s office ordered searches of homes and offices in Berlin and Brandenburg. These included rooms in the district administration, but only in connection with the two suspects. Investigators are now examining the documents and data they found. They say the administration is cooperating well with them. Since the Berlin scandal, the state office and the Brandenburg authorities have regularly exchanged information. Both federal states are now also working together to combat undeclared work.
