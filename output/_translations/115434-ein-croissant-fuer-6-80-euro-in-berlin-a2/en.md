A croissant for 6.80 euros in Berlin

The café “Tobi” in Berlin-Wilmersdorf sells special croissants. A croissant with salmon, ham or goat cheese costs 6.80 euros. Some guests think this price is very high.

Oleg Cherednychenko and his wife Hanna Nenno have had the café for a year. They came to Germany from Ukraine in 2022. Oleg bakes in the kitchen, and Hanna makes coffee for the guests. Oleg says: The quality is good, and we make almost everything ourselves.

The couple buys only fresh ingredients. The flour comes from France. A croissant takes almost two days of work. Oleg puts dough and butter on top of each other in twelve thin layers. Between each step, the dough often has to rest for a long time.

But the staff cost the most money. Oleg has twelve employees, and they earn about 18 euros per hour. He also has to pay rent and electricity bills.

The group of bakers in Germany says: Staff are often almost half of the costs. The minimum wage is the lowest pay per hour in Germany. It will rise from 13.90 euros to 14.60 euros in January 2027. Rents in Berlin have also become much more expensive since 2016. Electricity costs more today than in 2021.

So far, no guest has complained to Oleg. Neighbors, Ukrainians and many young people like to come. The young people often know the café from Instagram, an app for photos. Business is going well. That is why Oleg has now opened a second bakery in Friedrichshain.
