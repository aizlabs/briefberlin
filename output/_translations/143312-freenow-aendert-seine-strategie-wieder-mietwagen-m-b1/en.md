The Hamburg-based ride-hailing provider Freenow is changing its strategy. The company plans to arrange private hire cars with drivers again soon in Berlin and other German cities. Freenow withdrew from this business less than two years ago. Since January 2025, the app had offered only taxi rides in Berlin. The reason at the time was that prices were too low for the business to be profitable and encouraged illegal practices. The return has now been announced by David Risher, head of the US company Lyft. Lyft bought the former Mytaxi in 2025 and plans to gradually replace the Freenow name.

Freenow manager Alexander Mönch explains the move this way: Many customers want to save money, and a taxi ride is too expensive for them. Taxis will remain available, but Freenow does not want to leave the market for cheap rides to Uber and Bolt. Yet it was Mönch himself who issued a clear warning in 2024 to the transport committee of Berlin's parliament. After taxes, fixed costs and platform fees, ride-hailing companies have too little money left to pay the minimum wage and social security contributions, he said at the time. Their extremely low prices also threatened the taxi industry. This is because taxis have fixed fares, while private hire cars have no minimum price. A computer program calculates their prices according to supply and demand.

Even so, Mönch still stands by his earlier statements. He believes the business has since become cleaner. For example, the platforms no longer accept forged licences. Demand has also risen sharply. As a result, the cars receive new bookings almost without a break. But it remains questionable whether the industry is really free of crime. The public prosecutor's office in Neuruppin is investigating two employees of the Teltow-Fläming district administration in Brandenburg. They are suspected of taking money from private hire companies and making it easier for them to obtain licences in return. They are presumed innocent until a court reaches a verdict.

An expert report prepared for Berlin's transport administration in early 2026 also casts doubt on the business model. Referring to the customs authorities, it says that the low prices are possible only because social security contributions go unpaid and people work without being registered. A study commissioned by Uber, on the other hand, reached a different conclusion in autumn 2024. According to the study, app-based ride-hailing companies make a profit on average because their cars are kept busy. Mönch now believes it is up to the cities to act. He says they should set minimum prices for private hire cars to make competition with taxis fairer.
