The Hanover public prosecutor’s office will soon be able to investigate Steffen Krach again. Krach is the SPD’s state leader in Berlin. Hendrikje Klein of the party Die Linke, the president of the Berliner Abgeordnetenhaus, has given her consent. A spokeswoman for the state parliament announced the decision. The investigation had been stopped since Krach became a member of the new parliament. As a member of parliament, he has immunity. This means he is protected from criminal prosecution unless parliament lifts that protection. The public prosecutor’s office applied to lift his immunity on 3 November. But the letter did not arrive in Berlin until last week.

This is normally a routine process. The rules of the Berlin parliament say that investigations into members may continue after 48 hours if the president does not object. Klein had the case reviewed. According to the spokeswoman, there was no reason to object. At first, it was not clear exactly when the time limit would end. In parliament, the legal affairs committee is responsible for questions about immunity. It only has to make an official decision if the public prosecutor’s office plans further action, such as bringing charges in court.

The accusations concern Krach’s former work in Lower Saxony. At the time, he was president of Region Hannover. In that role, he was also automatically head of the supervisory board of Klinikum Region Hannover. This body oversees the work of the hospital company. The former hospital site in Lehrte was to be sold, and several interested parties made offers. Investigators are now examining whether Krach passed on secret information from this process. He is also suspected of accepting improper benefits in two cases. Krach denies all the accusations.

The affair also involves other SPD politicians in Lower Saxony. The public prosecutor’s office is investigating two other Social Democrats for aiding and abetting, meaning helping someone commit a crime. Until recently, Stefan Politze was head of the SPD parliamentary group in the state parliament in Hanover. He resigned in early October because of the accusations. Adis Ahmetović, a member of the Bundestag, is alleged to have helped restrict competition in bidding processes through secret agreements. He is also alleged to have violated business secrets. Politze and Ahmetović also deny the accusations. For Krach as Berlin SPD leader, the new investigation therefore adds to the pressure on him.
