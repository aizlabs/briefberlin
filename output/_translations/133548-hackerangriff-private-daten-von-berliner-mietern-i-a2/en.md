In August, criminals attacked the computers of Berlin’s city offices. They stole a lot of data. Now it is clear: The data includes private information about almost 17,000 tenants.

The data comes from two offices, one for transport and one for building. The criminals gave the city a deadline. After the deadline, they put the data on the dark web. This is a hidden part of the internet. Many applications from the years 2016 to 2020 are now there. People use these applications to get money for their rent. They live in social housing. These are cheap homes for people with little money.

The applications contain names, dates of birth and addresses. They often also include the rent and income. For 2423 people, they say: They have a serious disability. The data also includes bank details for almost 4000 people. Some people also wrote about illnesses, depression or family members who had died. One man wrote: Without my flat, I no longer have a home.

A single email is probably the reason for the problem. In 2020, a bank took over the work with the applications. After that, a city office worker sent three tables with the data to a colleague. Nobody knows why. That is why the data was on the city’s computer network. It is still unclear: Did the city tell the tenants?

A lawyer says: The city made a serious mistake in protecting the data. Each person can get several thousand euros. For families, it can easily be more than 10,000 euros. The lawyer believes: Many tenants will go to court together. But the money will not bring the private data back.
