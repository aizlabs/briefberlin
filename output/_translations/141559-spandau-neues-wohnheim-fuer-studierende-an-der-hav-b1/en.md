Construction has resumed after a long pause on the banks of the Havel in Berlin-Spandau. A student residence is being built between the Havel, the town hall tower and the bridge for ICE and commuter trains. Around 300 young people are expected to live here in furnished apartments. Construction began in 2022 and was originally supposed to be completed in 2024. But work on the construction site stopped repeatedly. The housing company Berlinovo, which belongs to the state of Berlin, pulled out of the project. Work resumed only in the summer, after a pause of more than a year. Everything is now supposed to be finished in 2027.

With the restart, there is also a new owner. The company Home & Co from Berlin-Mitte has bought the project. It offers small apartments for students in Germany and Spain. The complex has buildings of three to five floors and is now called Berlin Riverside. The company promotes it as an international community. There is, for example, a shared kitchen, a fitness room, a video game room and a conservatory. But living by the water is not cheap: a standard 18-square-metre apartment costs 829 euros a month. The largest apartment, under the roof, measures 33 square metres and costs 1,019 euros.

According to the company, renting will begin in April 2027. But it remains uncertain whether the complex will be finished by then. The third section of the building is still missing. The garden will probably not be finished by spring either. Even so, the location is very good. A pedestrian bridge leads to Bahnhof Spandau and the old town, with its shops and cafés, in just a few minutes. From there, it is easy to reach the universities in Berlin and Potsdam. A public path along the riverbank is also planned for later.

The plot of land has a long history. Until about 150 years ago, a cemetery belonging to St. Nikolai church was located here. Before construction began, the bones of the dead were dug up and buried again at the church. For years, the pieces of a 1980 fountain by the artist Ursula Sax also lay on the site. The original plan was to rebuild it in the courtyard. It is unclear whether that still applies after the sale. The artwork has apparently disappeared in the meantime.
