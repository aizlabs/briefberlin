A protest in support of Palestinians sparked a debate about security at the Bundestag on Sunday morning. A 22-year-old man climbed a flagpole almost 30 metres high outside the Reichstag building in Berlin. He replaced the German unity flag with a Palestinian flag. The German flag fell to the ground.

According to Berlin police, the man stayed on the pole for more than an hour. Two other people are said to have helped keep him safe as he climbed. The fire brigade tried to reach him using a ladder on an emergency vehicle. After he climbed down, police initially detained all three to check their identities. The climber remained in police custody afterwards. The Palestinian flag was removed. The pole's mechanism had apparently been damaged. After a temporary repair, the German flag was flying there again.

The state security police, a police department, are now investigating all three people involved. Among other things, they are suspected of insulting the state and its symbols. Police are also examining whether they damaged something and entered someone else's property without permission. Police consider the action to be a demonstration that was not registered in advance. They are therefore also examining whether those involved broke a law on public gatherings.

Berlin police protect the Reichstag building from the outside. They said that the area around the building is generally open to everyone. The barriers there help visitors find their way. Police do not have enough staff to guard the flagpole itself permanently. They now plan to review their security measures. Bundestag President Julia Klöckner is calling for better protection and also sees the state of Berlin as responsible. Berlin's governing mayor, Kai Wegner, condemned the action as disrespect for democracy.

CDU politician Thorsten Frei suggested considering stricter rules for protests around parliament. There is already a special protected area there. Gatherings are allowed only under certain conditions. An important factor is whether they disrupt the work of the Bundestag or its political groups. A stricter protection zone existed until 1999 around the former seat of parliament in Bonn.
