On Friday morning, the Galeria group announced that it was closing its historic department store on Kurfürstendamm in Berlin. The store then remained closed all day, and only notices on the door informed customers. Earlier, employees had learned of the closure at a meeting. They were disappointed and uncertain, so they were unable to work normally. The department store has been open again since Saturday, and sales are expected to continue until February. What will happen to the employees after that is unclear.

Across the six floors, there is little sign that the end is near. The goods are on display as usual, along with special offers. Many customers are already arriving by late morning, as the company apparently still wants to benefit from Christmas shopping. But the mood among the staff is very bad, one sales assistant says. A young colleague sees few opportunities, because other department stores are closing too. She wants to leave Berlin anyway. The trade union Verdi sharply criticizes the closures: for decades, the company has invested very little in its stores. Although employees gave up part of their wages for years, the company’s management did not acknowledge this. Now the insolvency administrator, who is managing the bankrupt company, is expected to find an investor for all 83 branches.

Many customers are sad too. The store opened in 1971 and was formerly called Wertheim, then later Karstadt. It was always among the department stores with the highest sales in Germany. A local resident therefore believes that the bosses made mistakes. A Berlin woman calls it a sad day for Ku'damm. Peek & Cloppenburg on nearby Tauentzienstraße also appears to be facing closure. Visitors from Schleswig-Holstein like how easy it is to find their way around department stores and the good advice they receive. They fear that city centres will become increasingly empty.

There are already new plans for the site. A Lidl supermarket next door and the pet supply chain Fressnapf will remain for now, as Fressnapf’s contract still has five years to run. Galeria’s lease, on the other hand, ends in spring 2027. The owner, Stumpf Development, apparently wants to build something new there: a high-rise and smaller buildings for apartments and shops.
