On Monday, hundreds of schools in several federal states received bomb threats by email, including 14 schools in Berlin. Berlin police initially reported more than 20 schools but later corrected the number. Police learned of the threats at around 9:30 a.m. In the almost identical emails, unknown senders wrote that they had hidden bombs in schools and wanted to set them off. However, the authorities see no specific danger and do not consider the threats serious.

Even so, the scale is large. In Baden-Württemberg, more than 450 schools received the emails, around 260 in Saxony-Anhalt and more than 170 in Brandenburg. Schools in Lower Saxony, North Rhine-Westphalia, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saxony, Schleswig-Holstein and Thuringia were also affected, as well as several schools in Denmark. The education ministry in Brandenburg said that schools were following their emergency plans. Some schools evacuated their buildings as a precaution, but most quickly returned to normal. The ministry advises schools to continue teaching as long as the police at the scene do not assess the situation differently.

In Berlin, the education department said there were initially no problems with the normal school day. Police are checking each case individually and are in contact with school management teams. They are also offering specially trained officers as contacts. A primary school in Neukölln only learned of the threat through the school authority because the email apparently went to the wrong address. There were hardly any children at the school anyway because of parent-teacher meetings. Stephan Weh, the Berlin head of the Gewerkschaft der Polizei, sharply criticised the threats. In his view, they are intended to spread fear and make societies feel unsafe. Nevertheless, he stressed that police must always check such emails thoroughly.

The case is part of a whole series. About two weeks ago, around 6,300 schools in Italy received bomb threats by email. Many buildings were evacuated, but nothing happened. Hundreds of schools were also affected in Scotland, Northern Ireland and Ireland. Italy's education minister, Giuseppe Valditara, called for caution at the time. He spoke of hybrid warfare, meaning hidden attacks intended to weaken countries. In Berlin, a threat of attack had already caused concern at around 20 schools in November 2025. It first appeared on a Russian-language Telegram channel.
