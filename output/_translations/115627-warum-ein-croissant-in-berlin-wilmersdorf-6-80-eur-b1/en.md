Croissants are very popular at Tobi café on Güntzelstraße in Berlin-Wilmersdorf. They come in savory versions with salmon, ham or goat cheese, or sweet versions with pistachio cream or chocolate. However, a savory croissant costs 6.80 euros. Oleg Cherednychenko and his wife Hanna Nenno have run the small café for a year. The couple came to Germany from Ukraine in 2022. He makes the dough in the bakery, while she takes care of the coffee and the customers. The owner says the price reflects good ingredients, a lot of work by hand and, above all, staff costs.

The café makes the dough and some of the creams itself. Cherednychenko uses fresh ingredients, such as flour from France and special butter from Germany. He says the work takes almost two days until the croissant is ready. Folding the dough is especially difficult. This creates twelve even layers of dough and butter, which make the croissant flaky and crisp. In between, the dough has to rest for long periods. The baker deliberately avoids frozen ready-made products.

The biggest expense, however, is the twelve employees, who earn about 18 euros an hour. According to Cherednychenko, up to 40 percent of the price goes toward staff. The Zentralverband des Deutschen Bäckerhandwerks, the organization representing bakeries, confirms that staff normally account for 40 to 50 percent of all costs. There are also machines, packaging and transport, as well as seating and service in the café. Many people underestimate these costs. In addition, the minimum wage will rise from 13.90 to 14.60 euros in January 2027. As a result, trained bakers often get paid more too. Rents in Berlin have risen from 8.93 to 15.80 euros per square meter since 2016, an increase of almost 77 percent. Electricity now costs 37.30 cents per kilowatt-hour, much more than in 2021.

The association stresses, however, that each business sets its own prices. Competition and the amount customers are willing to spend also play a role. Cherednychenko has not received any complaints so far. His customers include neighbors and Ukrainians who want to support the couple. On weekends, many young people also come from other districts because they discovered the café on Instagram. Business appears to be going well: a second café recently opened in Friedrichshain.
