Hackers attacked Berlin's city government. They stole data on more than 540,000 people. A new investigation by the newspaper Tagesspiegel shows this.

Berlin's government is called Senat. The Senat still does not know exactly how big the problem is. The data is now on the dark web. This is a hidden part of the internet. The newspaper believes there are probably even more people.

Not all the people in the data come from Berlin. Some live in other parts of Germany or in other countries. Their names are in tables, emails and lists.

There is at least one email address for more than 370,000 people. These are addresses from companies and city government workers, but also private addresses. There is more information on about 260,000 people. For example, you can see the date of birth of more than 157,000 people.

There are also around 34,000 mobile phone numbers in the data. Criminals can use these numbers to send false messages. They want to use them to steal money or passwords. You can even see the private bank account numbers of almost 14,000 people.

This is especially bad for at least 1,000 people. Information about their health is on the internet. For example, there are letters from doctors or information about a disability. There are also papers about going back to work after a long illness.

On the Tagesspiegel website, you can use your own email address to check: Am I affected too? The Senat will probably have to do more now. It should investigate the attack itself and inform people.
