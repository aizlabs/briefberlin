The Die Linke parliamentary group in Berlin’s Abgeordnetenhaus has expelled lawmaker Vanessa Emde. Members voted at a closed meeting on Tuesday afternoon. Thirty-three voted in favour, six against, and four abstained. Emde had faced criticism for some time because she had made hostile remarks about Israel.

The motion was submitted two weeks earlier by the parliamentary group’s leadership, including lead candidate Elif Eralp. Before that, there had been a long dispute within the group. One reason was a statement against antisemitism, or hatred of Jews. Emde signed it in mid-October, but publicly withdrew her signature two days later. The group now has only 43 lawmakers. A possible alliance of Die Linke, SPD and Grüne would still have a majority of 13 votes.

Emde sees her expulsion as the result of a media campaign against her. She also accuses her former parliamentary group of giving in to pressure from SPD and Grüne. She nevertheless intends to keep her seat in parliament. She says her supporters should not leave the party, but organise themselves. At the end of her statement, she called for class struggle and freedom for Palestine.

SPD and Grüne welcomed the decision. They had previously said they would not form a coalition as long as Emde was a member of the Die Linke parliamentary group. SPD state leader Steffen Krach spoke of an “important gain of ground in the fight against antisemitism”, meaning an important success. He was referring to an earlier statement by Emde. She had called her admission to the parliamentary group a gain of ground against the principle of national interest. This refers to Germany’s special responsibility for Israel’s security. Krach and his co-chair Bettina König said that Emde had put considerable strain on the talks. The fight against antisemitism and the protection of Jewish life are now to be included in the coalition agreement. Hakan Demir, the SPD member of the Bundestag for Neukölln, was relieved. Grüne politician Werner Graf said that antisemitism related to Israel has no place in a future government.

Josef Schuster, president of Zentralrat der Juden, was more cautious. For him, the expulsion is only a first step, because Emde is not an isolated case. He is therefore calling for further action, both during the coalition talks and afterwards.
